### Introduction to a Bygone Era
The 1990s was a transformative period for college football, with the sport undergoing significant changes that would shape its future. However, as with any era, there are aspects that are better left in the past. In this article, we will delve into ten things we don’t miss about college football in the 1990s, from outdated rules to lackluster fan experiences.

### The Dark Ages of Safety Protocols
One of the most glaring omissions of 1990s college football was the lack of emphasis on player safety. The decade saw a plethora of brutal hits and injuries that would be deemed unacceptable by today’s standards. The introduction of stricter safety protocols and rule changes has significantly reduced the number of serious injuries in the sport.

### Outdated Equipment and Gear
The 1990s were marked by subpar equipment and gear, which often put players at risk. From ill-fitting helmets to inadequate padding, the decade was a hotbed for preventable injuries. Thankfully, advancements in technology have led to the development of safer, more effective equipment.

### Limited Television Coverage
College football fans in the 1990s often had to rely on sparse television coverage, with many games going untelevised. The advent of cable television and online streaming has revolutionized the way we consume college football, providing unparalleled access to games and analysis.

### The Absence of Instant Replay
The 1990s were a time when referees’ decisions were often final, with no recourse for disputing erroneous calls. The introduction of instant replay has greatly increased the accuracy of officiating, reducing controversy and ensuring a fairer game.

### Antiquated Recruiting Practices
The recruiting process in the 1990s was often shrouded in mystery, with coaches and players engaging in questionable practices. Modern recruiting has become more transparent, with the NCAA implementing stricter guidelines to prevent exploitation and ensure a level playing field.

### Lackluster Fan Experiences
Stadiums in the 1990s often lacked the amenities and atmosphere that modern fans take for granted. From cramped seating to limited concessions, the overall experience was often underwhelming. Contemporary stadiums have raised the bar, offering a wide range of amenities and activities to enhance the fan experience.

### Inadequate Training Facilities
Many college football programs in the 1990s were hindered by subpar training facilities, which put them at a competitive disadvantage. The construction of state-of-the-art training facilities has become a staple of successful programs, providing players with the resources they need to excel.

### Insufficient Academic Support
The 1990s saw a lack of emphasis on academic support for college football players, with many athletes struggling to balance their athletic and academic responsibilities. Modern programs have placed a greater emphasis on academic support, providing players with the resources they need to succeed in the classroom.

### Limited Media Coverage
The 1990s were a time when media coverage of college football was limited, with few outlets dedicated to providing in-depth analysis and news. The rise of online media has created a plethora of outlets for fans to stay informed, with dedicated websites, podcasts, and social media channels offering unparalleled coverage.

### A Bygone Era Comes to a Close
As we reflect on the college football landscape of the 1990s, it is clear that the sport has undergone significant transformations. While there are certainly aspects of the era that are nostalgic, there are many more that are better left in the past.
